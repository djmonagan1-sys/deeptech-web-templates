The technology

Atoms make the best sensors. We made them practical.

Our sensors read the quantum state of atoms and engineered defects in diamond. Because the reference is a law of nature — not a manufactured part — they don't drift, don't need recalibration, and reach sensitivities classical electronics can't touch.

Optically pumped magnetometry

Polarized laser light prepares an atomic vapor; tiny magnetic fields rotate its spin. We read that rotation to detect fields a billion times weaker than Earth's.

Nitrogen-vacancy diamond

Atomic defects in synthetic diamond fluoresce differently under magnetic stress. Solid-state, room-temperature, and rugged enough for the field.

Cold-atom interferometry

Laser-cooled atoms in free fall form an inertial reference of extraordinary stability — gravimetry and navigation that never accumulate error.

  1. 01

    Prepare

    Lasers cool and polarize atoms into a known quantum state.

  2. 02

    Interrogate

    The field of interest perturbs the state in a precisely measurable way.

  3. 03

    Read out

    Photodetectors measure the shift; DSP converts it to a calibrated value.

  4. 04

    Deliver

    A clean digital stream — no drift, no warm-up, no recalibration.
